# Research brief: how the Disha site should talk to a homebuyer

October 2026. Four strands: buyer psychology, web trust and conversion evidence, the Bengaluru market and competitors, and an audit of the current site. Evidence strength is noted as strong, moderate or weak; claims without evidence are marked as folk wisdom and are not used.

## What the evidence says

### The buyer
- A home is bought mainly on practical grounds, but feeling still adds a real effect (Andrew & Larceneux 2019, moderate). "Buying a home is 90% emotional" is folk wisdom.
- Home is attachment to people and routine as much as to the building (Lewicka 2011, review of about 400 studies, strong). Community is the strongest wellbeing lever: social ties raise survival odds 1.5 times (Holt-Lunstad 2010, strong).
- Imagining yourself using something raises the sense of ownership and intent to buy (Escalas 2004; Peck et al. 2013, moderate).
- Losses loom large in housing (Genesove & Mayer 2001, strong). The buyer's fear is of a costly, irreversible mistake.
- Indian buyers' top demands of a developer: on-time completion 98%, construction quality 93%, ventilation 72% (Anarock H1 2025, moderate). In Bengaluru the top purchase factors are price, builder reputation and location (Knight Frank 2025, moderate). 71% of Bengaluru buyers prefer under-construction homes, which only works when they trust delivery.
- Bengaluru-specific anxieties: delays (about 1,300 delayed RERA projects in the city), water (Mahadevapura, Varthur and Whitefield were worst hit in 2024), e-Khata confusion, and commute (about 36 minutes per 10 km).
- Green space is linked to lower stress and better outcomes for children (Twohig-Bennett & Jones 2018, strong for the association). Night noise harms sleep (WHO 2018, strong). "Neuroscience-designed homes" claims are folk wisdom.

### Persuasion
- Stories that absorb the reader change beliefs (Green & Brock 2000; van Laer 2014 meta-analysis, strong).
- An underdog origin story raises purchase intent through identification (Paharia et al. 2011, moderate to strong). Disha's version: "we were homebuyers first".
- Concrete, specific language beats abstract claims (Packard & Berger 2021, moderate to strong).
- Highly involved buyers respond to facts more than to testimonials (Braverman 2008, moderate). A home is the most involved purchase there is, so every story must sit next to proof.
- Ratings of 4.2 to 4.7 outperform a perfect 5.0. Reviews matter most for expensive purchases (Spiegel Research Center, moderate).

### The page
- First impressions of visual appeal form in about 50 ms and tend to stick (Lindgaard 2006, strong). The aesthetic-usability effect is strong for perceived ease of use.
- NN/g's four trust factors: design quality, upfront disclosure (prices and terms), current and complete content, and links to outside proof.
- 57% of viewing time is above the fold and 74% in the first two screens (NN/g 2018). People scan headings and numbers.
- Real photographs of people and places are studied; decorative and stock images are ignored. Renders must be labelled.
- Auto-rotating carousels are largely ignored. Use one primary action per screen and a compact sticky action on mobile.
- White space signals quality (Pracejus et al. 2006). Universal colour-psychology rules are a myth; choose colour for contrast and brand.
- A face looking towards the content draws the eye to it (Sajjacholapunt & Ball 2014).
- The peak-end rule (strong clinically; applying it to pages is an inference): end each page on reassurance plus one easy action.
- About 66% of Indian traffic is mobile. Speed affects conversion: CPU and script weight matter more than bandwidth.

## Where the current site falls short
- It talks about the company, not the buyer: the Approach, Why Disha, house-count, concert and manifesto sections.
- It never answers the buyer's real questions:
  - price;
  - possession date;
  - carpet areas and floor plans;
  - specifications for Elements;
  - commute times;
  - water;
  - loans;
  - legal status of Disha's own projects.
- The ET Business Award 2025 for Elements sits only on the Story page's gig wall.
- Its pages contradict themselves:
  - handover dates fall before completion dates;
  - two month counts are wrong;
  - "1,102 homes" sits beside "1,000+ families" and "1,000-plus members";
  - square footage delivered is shown as 20,25,000+ while the data adds up to about 19,95,000;
  - "studio homes" are mentioned but don't exist.
- "We'll call you back" is promised, but nothing is sent until the visitor sends it.
- Sold-out communities end in "Book a site visit", and Elements is missing from the comparison table.
- The same paragraph and the same "coffee" line repeat across four places.

## Positioning gaps Disha can own
1. A delivery ledger: promised date against actual date for every project, with RERA links. Competitors only say "trusted".
2. Being honest about water in East Bengaluru.
3. Paperwork peace of mind: OC, Khata and carpet-area status for Disha's own projects.
4. Founders you can reach: "two homebuyers who became builders, and still answer the phone".
5. Life after handover: resident ratings (Central Park scores 4.7 on MyGate) and community.
